# Isolation of DNA from Coconut Endosperm

A practical laboratory protocol and overview for extracting and isolating genomic DNA from coconut (*Cocos nucifera*) endosperm using organic liquid-liquid extraction.

---

## Aim
To isolate and extract genomic DNA from coconut endosperm using the liquid-liquid DNA extraction method.

---

## Scientific Principle

This protocol utilizes an organic **liquid-liquid extraction method** leveraging differences in solubility and polarity:

1. **Cell Lysis & Binding Neutralization:**
   * Lysis buffer disrupts cell membranes and releases nuclear contents into solution.
   * **Sodium Acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$)** neutralizes the negative charges on the phosphate backbone of DNA, enabling molecules to aggregate.
2. **Phase Separation (Phenol-Chloroform):**
   * **DNA** is highly polar with a net negative charge, making it soluble in the upper aqueous phase.
   * **Phenol** denatures proteins, exposing hydrophobic regions that partition into the denser bottom organic phase.
   * Two distinct immiscible phases form: protein-rich organic phase (bottom) and DNA-rich aqueous phase (top).
3. **Precipitation:**
   * Adding **ice-cold absolute ethanol** drastically reduces DNA solubility, precipitating visible, white thread-like DNA strands out of solution.

---

## Reagents & Materials Required

### Biological Material
* Fresh Coconut Endosperm ($\approx 250\text{ mg}$)

### Equipment & Tools
* Mortar and pestle
* Centrifuge
* Test tubes / Microcentrifuge tubes
* Glass rod (for spooling)

### Chemical Reagents & Buffers
* **Lysis Buffer** ($100\text{ mL}$ total volume, adjusted to $\text{pH } 5.0$):
  * $2\text{ g}$ CTAB / TAB
  * $10\text{ mL}$ $1\text{ M}$ Tris ($\text{pH } 8.0$)
  * $4\text{ mL}$ $0.5\text{ M}$ EDTA ($\text{pH } 8.0$)
  * $28\text{ mL}$ $5\text{ M}$ $\text{NaCl}$
  * $1\text{ g}$ PVP40
  * $40\text{ mL}$ Distilled Water
* **Phenol : Chloroform** ($1:1\text{ v/v}$)
* **Chloroform : Isopropanol** ($24:1\text{ v/v}$)
* **Sodium Acetate** ($7.5\text{ M}$)
* **Ice-cold Absolute Ethanol**
* **TE Buffer** (Tris-EDTA, for final storage and downstream quantification)

---

## Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Homogenization:**
   * Take $\approx 250\text{ mg}$ of coconut endosperm and homogenize using a mortar and pestle with lysis buffer.
   * Adjust final volume to $10\text{ mL}$.

2. **Incubation:**
   * Incubate the homogenized mixture at $50^\circ\text{C}$ for $5\text{ minutes}$.

3. **First Phase Separation:**
   * Add $5\text{ mL}$ of Phenol:Chloroform mixture ($1:1$).
   * Centrifuge at $3,000\text{ rpm}$ for $10\text{ minutes}$.

4. **Second Phase Separation:**
   * Carefully collect the top aqueous supernatant.
   * Add Chloroform:Isopropanol mixture ($24:1$).
   * Centrifuge at $5,000\text{ rpm}$ for $3\text{ minutes}$.

5. **Precipitation:**
   * Transfer the upper aqueous layer to a fresh tube.
   * Add $0.1\text{ mL}$ of $7.5\text{ M}$ sodium acetate and $2\text{ mL}$ of ice-cold absolute ethanol.

6. **Recovery:**
   * Observe white thread-like precipitates of DNA forming immediately.
   * Spool the DNA using a glass rod and dissolve in TE buffer for subsequent quantification.

---

## Observations & Results

* Upon addition of cold absolute ethanol, white, thread-like strands of precipitated DNA appeared almost immediately.
* The precipitated nucleic acid was successfully spooled using a glass rod.

| Phase / Step | Visual Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| **First Centrifugation** | Partial separation of lysate layers. |
| **Second Centrifugation** | Clear formation of two immiscible phases (Aqueous layer on top, Organic layer below). |
| **Ethanol Addition** | Immediate precipitation of white, fibrous DNA strands. |

---

## Conclusion
DNA strands were successfully extracted and isolated from coconut endosperm using liquid-liquid organic extraction, followed by precipitation with sodium acetate and cold ethanol, and prepared for storage in TE buffer.
